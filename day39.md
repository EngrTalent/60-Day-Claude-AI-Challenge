The best privacy feature isn't a setting, it's an architecture decision.

For Day 39 of my ABTalks 60-Day Claude AI Mastery Challenge, I built Leaflet, a fully client-side PDF splitter and merger that processes documents entirely in the browser.
No uploads.
No servers.
No waiting for your files to leave your device before you can use them.

At first, I thought the biggest benefit would be privacy, it was.
But it wasn't the only one.
The moment I removed the traditional "Uploading..." step, the entire experience felt different.

There was no progress bar to watch.
No wondering where your document was being stored.
No uncertainty about what happened after you clicked "Upload."

Your files stayed on your device, and the results appeared almost instantly.
That completely changed how I think about privacy-focused software for real.

Privacy isn't just about protecting data, it's about removing unnecessary moments of doubt.
Building the app also reminded me that the smallest details often require the most careful thinking📌.

Handling page ranges wasn't as simple as it looked.
Overlapping selections, empty ranges, and "split after page" rules all needed clear feedback instead of a generic error message. Because good validation isn't just a coding challenge, it's part of the user experience.

Today's project reinforced one lesson I'll carry into future builds:
The best privacy features aren't the ones users have to turn on.
They're the ones built into the architecture from the very beginning.
That's when privacy stops being an option and becomes the default.
