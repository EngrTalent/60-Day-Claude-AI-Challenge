Most people don't fall for lies.
They fall for framing.
That realization inspired this project.

For Day 33 of my ABTalks 60-Day Claude AI Mastery Challenge, I built a Media Integrity Analyzer, an interactive learning tool designed to help people recognize manipulation before it shapes their thinking.

Instead of asking users to memorize facts or answer another quiz, I wanted them to experience how easily emotions can influence judgment.

The experience begins with a headline or social media post and asks two simple questions:
Would you click this?
How does it make you feel?

Only after you've made your decision does the simulator reveal what's happening beneath the surface.
It analyzes how accurately the headline reflects the story, highlights emotionally loaded language, identifies common manipulation techniques, and even rewrites the message in a more balanced and factual way.

Actually, the biggest surprise wasn't the technology.
It was realizing how predictable manipulation becomes once you start looking for patterns.
Fear.
Urgency.
Scarcity.
Outrage.

Different headlines, but the same psychological techniques.
That completely changed how I approached the learning experience.

Instead of explaining media literacy first, I wanted users to experience the emotional pull before revealing the analysis.
You know some people rarely remember what they're told, they remember what they discover for themselves.

The biggest lesson I took away from this project wasn't about AI.
It was about critical thinking🔥.

Media literacy isn't about knowing more facts, it's about recognizing the techniques before they shape your thinking.
That's a skill that matters far beyond the internet.
