I used to think building better AI was mostly about writing better prompts.
Do you know what, this project actually changed my mind.

I realized the real challenge is building a better mental model first📌.

For Day 31 of my ABTalks 60-Day Claude AI Mastery Challenge, I built an AI Supply Chain Control Tower
An interactive simulation where you're the Head of Operations responding to real-time disruptions.

Imagine this:
🚢 A port suddenly becomes congested.
🏭 A factory goes offline.
📦 Customer demand spikes overnight.
⏳ You have three minutes to respond.

Every decision has consequences.
▫️Expedite a shipment?
Your costs increase.
▫️Ignore the disruption?
Customer satisfaction drops.
▫️Overreact?
Inventory suffers.

While building the simulator, I expected the hardest part to be the code.
But it wasn't actually✨.

The hardest part was deciding what good judgment actually looks like😪.

Before Claude could simulate an experienced operations leader, I had to answer questions like:
What makes one decision better than another?
Which trade-offs matter most?
When is higher cost actually the smarter choice?

Only then could the AI turn those ideas into a working system.

That led me to the biggest lesson from today's build:
AI is only as sharp as the judgment you hand it.
Models don't magically understand good decision-making.
They reflect the quality of the thinking we encode into them.
The technology matters.
But the reasoning behind it matters even more😉.
