Do you know what, debugging isn't optional.
Even in a portfolio project, broken code is a broken lesson📌.

That realization completely changed today's build.
For Day 34 of my ABTalks 60-Day Claude AI Mastery Challenge, I built Marketing Detective, an interactive game where players investigate failed marketing campaigns, collect evidence, identify the real cause of failure, and close each case like a detective✨.

Here's what happened today ...the first version I built looked great😇.
But do you know what, there was just one problem🥹.
It didn't work😢.

Funniest part was that it was built with React via CDN, but then it was displaying a blank page😪.
No obvious errors for real, and I could have spent hours patching around the issue.

But then I made a different decision😌.
I rebuilt the entire project from scratch using nothing but vanilla HTML, CSS, and JavaScript✨.
No frameworks, no external dependencies.
Just one self-contained file that runs anywhere😇.

Ironically, the biggest lesson from today's project wasn't about marketing, it was about engineering😉.
I also learnt that the most impressive solution isn't always the most reliable one📌.
Sometimes the professional choice isn't adding another framework, it's removing one🤜🤛.

By the end, Marketing Detective became exactly what I wanted it to be, a fully offline, interactive learning experience where players solve 10 fictional marketing mysteries, connect evidence on a detective board, uncover the real reason each campaign failed, and walk away with practical lessons they can apply to future projects😉.

Today's build reminded me that users don't remember how elegant your code was, but they remember whether it worked🔥.

Day 34/60 of my Claude AI Mastery Challenge.
