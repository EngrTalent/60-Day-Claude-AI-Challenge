Most budget apps are designed for one user, and I wanted to build one that still made sense after you handed it to someone else.
That idea shaped today's project✨.

For Day 42 of my ABTalks 60-Day Claude AI Mastery Challenge, I built a Personal Financial Command Center designed for a very specific user: students living on a fixed allowance, and saving towards one meaningful goal.

Instead of overwhelming users with investment portfolios, tax planning, and income tracking, I focused on the decisions students actually make every week.
How much should I budget?
Am I still on track to reach my savings goal?
What happens if I save a little more... or a little less?

The dashboard includes budgeting envelopes, a savings runway, a financial health score, and a "what-if" simulator that lets users see how small changes today affect future progress.
The biggest surprise wasn't building the analytics, it was designing the reset system.

At first, I thought "reset" simply meant clearing the data.
Guess what it didn't😌.

A true reset meant someone else could pick up the same device, create their own financial plan, and later restore my original dashboard exactly as I left it✨.
That small requirement completely changed the architecture.
It forced me to stop thinking about a single user, and start thinking about shared experiences.

The biggest lesson I took away from today's project wasn't about personal finance, it was about product design🔥.

A product that only works for its creator isn't finished, it's only finished when someone else can use it with confidence, too😉.
Building for the second user made the first user's experience better as well.
And that's a design principle I'll carry into every project I build from here😇.

Day 42/60 of my Claude AI Mastery Challenge.
