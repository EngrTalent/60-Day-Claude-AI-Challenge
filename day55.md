The best user experiences are often the ones people never notice because when everything just works, no one thinks about the engineering behind it.

For Day 55/60 of my ABTalks 60-Day Claude AI Mastery Challenge, and Day 5 of building TeeTronics Gadgets Smart Match, I built the guided recommendation flow that learns what a customer actually needs before suggesting a laptop.

The experience walks users through seven simple questions, capturing their budget, primary use case, and preferences for features like touchscreen support, keyboard backlighting, fingerprint authentication, RAM, and processor requirements.

Every step is connected by a progress indicator and smooth Back/Next navigation.
Simple on the surface.
Much harder behind the scenes.

The biggest surprise wasn't designing the questions, it was managing the user's journey.

A recommendation flow isn't just about moving forward, it's about making sure users can move backward without losing their answers.

The moment someone taps Back and discovers their previous choices have disappeared, trust also disappears with them.

That made me realize something.
State management isn't just a technical concern, it's part of the user experience.

Another lesson came from testing.
The app looked perfect in my browser's mobile simulator, then I opened it on my actual phone.
Within minutes, I noticed interactions and small usability details that a resized desktop window never revealed.
And that reminded me that responsive design isn't just about making a browser narrower, it's also about understanding how people actually use their devices.

The biggest lesson I took away wasn't about multi-step forms, it was about product quality.

Never mistake "it works on my computer" for "it works for my users."

Real confidence comes from testing in the same environment where your product will actually be used.

By the end of the day, Smart Match had a fully working, real-device-tested recommendation flow that captures every answer needed for the next stage.
