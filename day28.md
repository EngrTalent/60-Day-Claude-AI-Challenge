I tried to "game" the AI.
Guess what, it refused😆.

That's the most impressive part of this project for real😌.

For Day 28 of my ABTalks 60-Day Claude AI Mastery Challenge, I built a Hospital Admission Readiness Simulator with Claude.

At first, I assumed that if every administrative task was completed, the simulator would eventually approve the admission.
But it didn't🤥.

Even after checking every box, one missing requirement kept the case from reaching an "Admit" decision.
Guess why?
Because I taught the system an important rule:
Some decisions should never depend on a simple average score.
If a patient's prior authorization is denied for a high-acuity case, completing paperwork shouldn't magically make them admission-ready.
The simulator had to respect that constraint.

That experience reminded me of something bigger about AI.

The most reliable systems aren't the ones with the most features.
Instead, they're the ones with the clearest rules📌.

Good AI isn't just about generating answers.
It's about knowing when not to give the answer people want.

And by the end of the project, I had built an interactive simulator that tracks readiness across multiple factors, explains why a case isn't ready, and highlights exactly what needs attention before moving forward.

More importantly, I walked away with a lesson I'll use in every future AI project:
Constraints don't make AI weaker.
They make it more trustworthy.

Day 28/60 of my Claude AI Mastery Challenge.
