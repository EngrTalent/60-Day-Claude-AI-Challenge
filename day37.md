Most workplace problems aren't solved by the smartest person.

They're solved by the right handoff.

That realization inspired today's build.

For Day 37 of my ABTalks 60-Day Claude AI Mastery Challenge, I built **Task Compass**—an interactive simulation that teaches how work actually moves through a tech company.

Instead of asking users to memorize job titles or responsibilities, the experience puts them in charge of a real task.

Who should handle it first?

Who needs the information next?

And when a major crisis hits, which teams need to work together to solve it?

The biggest surprise wasn't how people assigned ownership.

It was how they approached collaboration.

When a complex problem appeared, the instinct was almost always the same:

*"Who should I give this to?"*

But that's rarely how real organizations work.

Some challenges can't be solved by one person.

They require engineering, product, customer support, operations, and leadership to move in sync.

That completely changed how I thought about the simulator.

Instead of rewarding users for finding a single "correct" owner, I wanted it to reward them for understanding how work flows across teams.

The biggest lesson I took away from today's project wasn't about organizational structure.

It was about leadership.

**Ownership is important.**

**Delegation is necessary.**

**But collaboration is what keeps organizations moving.**

Because the best teams don't just know who is responsible.

They know **when to involve others and how to hand work over effectively.**
