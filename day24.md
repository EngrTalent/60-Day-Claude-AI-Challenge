Claude scored my business idea 30/100 on investor readiness. And honestly?
That was the most valuable feedback I've received so far.

As part of my startup-building journey with TeeTronics Gadgets, I decided to stop asking:
"Is this a good idea?"
And start asking:
"Can this become a sustainable business?"

So I fed my Customer & MVP Blueprint back into Claude and asked it to evaluate the business like an investor, growth strategist, and startup advisor would.

The results weren't exactly flattering:
📉 Investor Readiness: 30/100
📉 Go-To-Market Strength: 40/100
📉 Business Viability: 52/100

At first glance, those numbers look discouraging.
But one insight completely changed my perspective.

Claude ran a Reverse SWOT analysis and identified what I thought was my biggest weakness:
"I don't have enough capital to hold inventory🥲."

And instead of treating that as a problem, it reframed it as a potential advantage.
Why?
Because a pre-order model could allow me to validate demand before committing cash to stock.

In other words:
The thing I saw as a limitation could become part of the strategy😉.

That led to my biggest lesson from today:
A good business idea and a sustainable business are not the same thing.

The real question isn't:
"Does this solve a problem?"
The real question is:
"Will people consistently pay for this solution through a repeatable channel?"
And trust me, everything else is secondary😌.

So the verdict for TeeTronics Gadgets isn't:
❌ Reject
And it's not:
✅ Invest
It's:
🟡 Validate

My next job isn't building more plans.
It's talking to potential customers and finding out whether they'll actually pay for the value I'm promising.
