Writing code feels like progress.
Designing the system first doesn't.
Until you're halfway through a project and realize every early decision is coming back to haunt you🤥.

For Day 52/60 of my ABTalks Claude AI Mastery Challenge, and Day 2 of my 10-day capstone build, I didn't write a single feature. Instead, I built the foundation.

Before touching the application itself, I designed the complete technical blueprint for TeeTronics Gadgets Smart Match☺️.
That meant mapping the system architecture, designing the Firestore database schema, defining application functions, sketching UI wireframes, organizing the project structure, and documenting how everything connects before implementation begins🔥.
Once everything was in place, I committed the entire blueprint to GitHub.

The biggest surprise wasn't how much work went into planning, it was how much uncertainty disappeared afterward.
When every collection, function, and screen already has a clear purpose, building stops feeling like improvisation, it starts feeling like execution.
And that completely changed how I think about software projects.

Planning isn't something you do before the "real work," it is part of the real work.

The biggest lesson I took away wasn't about Firebase or Firestore, it was about engineering discipline.
A good system design isn't documentation for other people, it's a promise to your future self.
It's the difference between opening your laptop tomorrow wondering "Where do I even start?," and opening it already knowing exactly what needs to be built next.
That's the kind of momentum I'm trying to create with this capstone😉.

Less time making the same decisions twice.
More time building.

Day 52/60 of my Claude AI Mastery Challenge.
Day 2/10 of building TeeTronics Gadgets Smart Match.

The planning phase is complete, and now it's time to turn the blueprint into a working product.
