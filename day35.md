Something very common is that most people write prompts by guessing, and today's project is something that teaches why good prompts work.

For Day 35 of my ABTalks 60-Day Claude AI Mastery Challenge, I built Prompt Puzzle, an interactive game that helps beginners learn prompt engineering through play instead of trial and error😉.

Rather than asking users to memorize prompting tips, I challenged them to think like the AI😌.

So the idea is that players drag instruction blocks into the right order, simplify overcomplicated prompts, and compare weak, strong, and overly engineered prompts using real healthcare scenarios.
The biggest surprise wasn't how enjoyable the game became, it was how clearly the same pattern kept appearing.

Weak prompts fail because they don't give the model enough direction.
Over-engineered prompts fail because they bury the real task under unnecessary complexity.

The best prompts sit somewhere in the middle✨.
A clear role.
One specific task.
Just enough context.
And a simple constraint on the output.

Building the scoring system made me realize something else.
Prompt engineering isn't a talent you're born with, it's a skill you can practice, measure, and improve🔥.

The moment I stopped thinking about prompts as "magic words" and started thinking about them as clear communication, everything became easier to teach for real.

Today's project reminded me that the goal of prompt engineering isn't to impress an AI, it's to remove ambiguity.
Because the clearer your instructions are, the better your results.
