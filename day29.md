I thought I was building a supply chain simulator.
But I ended up learning a lesson about leadership.

For Day 29 of my ABTalks 60-Day Claude AI Mastery Challenge, I built an Operation Lifeline
It's an AI-powered crisis management simulator.

Every time you start the simulation, you're placed in charge of a different company facing a different crisis:
🔥 A factory fire
🚢 A port strike
💻 A cyberattack
...and many more.

At first, I thought the challenge would be writing the code.
But do you know what, it wasn't for real.

The real challenge was designing consequences.
Every decision had a cost.
Choose air freight to save delivery times?
Your profit margin drops.
Delay communication with customers?
Trust declines.
Invest too little in resilience?
The next crisis hits even harder.

That changed how I think about AI.
The smartest systems don't just generate answers.
They force us to think through trade-offs📌.

One insight stood out throughout the build:
Good simulation design is really just systems thinking brought to life.
Once I stopped asking Claude to "build a game" and instead asked it to "build a decision engine where every choice has consequences," the quality of the output changed dramatically.

That wasn't just a prompting lesson.
It was a leadership lesson☺️.

Because leadership is rarely about finding the perfect decisions.
It's about understanding what each decision will cost🔥.

Day 29/60 of my Claude AI Mastery Challenge.
