The "best" laptop doesn't exist but the best laptop for you does.
That realization inspired this project.

For Day 48 of my ABTalks 60-Day Claude AI Mastery Challenge, I built an interactive decision tool to help Nigerian students compare four of the most popular UK-used laptops on the market.

Instead of asking, "Which laptop is best?", I asked a better question:
"Best according to what?"
Before writing a single line of code, I interviewed Claude to define the exact requirements, researched real specifications, gathered current Jiji.ng pricing, and verified technical details from trusted hardware sources.

The result was a tool where users don't just compare laptops, they decide what matters most.
Need the lowest possible price? Increase the price weighting.
Need longer battery life for lectures? Prioritize battery.
Care more about performance than anything else? Adjust the sliders and watch the rankings change instantly.

The biggest surprise wasn't the interface, it was seeing how dramatically the recommendations changed.
A laptop that ranked first under one set of priorities could fall to the bottom under another.
Nothing about the laptops changed, only the person's priorities did.
That completely changed how I think about recommendation systems.

The goal isn't to tell people what to buy, it's to help them understand why one option fits them better than another.

And another principle became just as important while building it, every specification had to be traceable.
Whenever I relied on verified information, I cited the source.
Whenever an estimate was necessary, I labelled it as an estimate.
That distinction may seem small but it's the difference between asking someone to trust your opinion, and giving them enough evidence to make their own decision.

The biggest lesson I took away from this project wasn't about laptops, it was about trust.
Good products don't just give answers, they show people how those answers were reached.

And when you're helping someone spend their hard-earned money, transparency isn't an extra feature, it's the product.

Day 48/60 of my Claude AI Mastery Challenge.
