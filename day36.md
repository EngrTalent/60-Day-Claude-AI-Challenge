The best learning doesn't happen when people are given answers.
It happens when they discover the answers for themselves.
That realization shaped today's build.
For Day 36 of my ABTalks 60-Day Claude AI Mastery Challenge, I built Cognitive Pattern Explorer—an interactive experience that helps people reflect on how they make decisions rather than telling them what kind of thinker they are.
Instead of another personality quiz, I wanted to build something more thoughtful.
Users move through branching scenarios, organize priorities, and map out their thinking process. Only at the end does the experience reveal patterns that emerged from their own decisions.
The biggest challenge wasn't building the interactions.
It was choosing the right words.
My early versions sounded too certain, almost as if the app was diagnosing people.
That wasn't the goal.
So I rewrote every insight to encourage reflection instead of certainty.
Not:
"You are an overthinker."
But:
"Your responses suggest you may spend more time evaluating options before acting."
That small change made the experience feel less like it was judging users and more like it was helping them understand themselves.
It also reminded me of something bigger about designing AI-powered tools.
The goal isn't to label people.
It's to help them notice patterns they may not have seen before.
The more I worked on the scoring system, the more I realized that meaningful insights don't come from one answer.
They emerge from patterns across many decisions.
Today's build wasn't really about cognitive styles.
It was about designing technology that encourages curiosity instead of certainty.
Because sometimes the most valuable insight isn't the one an AI gives you.
It's the one it helps you discover for yourself.
