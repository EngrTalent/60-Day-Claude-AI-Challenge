A product doesn't become professional when you add more features, it becomes professional when you start fixing the things users notice before they ever tell you.

For Day 57/60 of my ABTalks 60-Day Claude AI Mastery Challenge, and Day 7 of building TeeTronics Gadgets Smart Match, I moved beyond the recommendation engine and focused on the experience around it.

I built customer accounts, allowing users to create an account, log in, save their laptop recommendations, and come back to them later.
At the same time, I made sure the core recommendation flow stayed completely accessible without requiring anyone to sign up first.
Then came something every product eventually needs...

A full UI/UX review.

I went through the app screen by screen, improving accessibility, refining loading states, smoothing transitions, and adding the small micro-interactions that make software feel responsive instead of mechanical. And the biggest surprise wasn't adding new functionality, it was discovering bugs that the code itself never revealed.

One appeared when I clicked Log In too quickly, nothing happened.
Another showed up after logging out ...the interface still displayed information that should have disappeared.

Reading the code never exposed those problems, but using the product did.
And that reminded me of something I don't want to forget ...Software isn't tested by reading it, it's tested by living inside it📌.

Another lesson came from accessibility.

Originally, the app relied on color alone to show which option a user had selected, and itt looked perfectly fine until I stopped thinking like the developer, and started thinking like someone who might not distinguish those colors easily.
Adding a simple checkmark transformed that interaction from decorative to inclusive.

And the biggest lesson I took away wasn't about authentication, it was about product quality🔥.

A feature isn't finished when it works, it's finished when it works reliably for everyone.

By the end of the day, Smart Match had a fully working account system, persistent recommendation history, smoother interactions throughout the app, and two real-world bugs eliminated through hands-on testing.
And feels noticeably closer to something I'd confidently hand to a real customer.

Day 57/60 of my Claude AI Mastery Challenge.
Day 7/10 of building TeeTronics Gadgets Smart Match.

Less time adding features.
More time making the existing ones feel right.
