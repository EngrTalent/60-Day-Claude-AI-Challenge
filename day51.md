The easiest way to kill a great product is to keep adding "just one more feature."
That realization kicked off the final phase of my ABTalks 60-Day Claude AI Mastery Challenge.

Day 51/60 also marks Day 1 of a 10-day capstone build.

Instead of creating another standalone AI project, I'm spending the final ten days building something designed to solve a real business problem for TeeTronics Gadgets.
The project is called Smart Match, an intelligent laptop recommendation tool that helps customers choose the right laptop based on their budget, needs, and priorities, while explaining why each recommendation fits them.

My first instinct was to build everything.
Laptop recommendations.
Phone recommendations.
Tablets.
Accessories.
An admin dashboard.
Inventory management.
The list kept growing.

Then I paused and asked myself one question:
What problem am I trying to solve first?
And that changed the direction of the entire project.

Instead of jumping straight into development, I spent the day doing structured discovery. Before writing a single line of code, I completed a product requirements document, mapped out a realistic nine-day implementation plan, and prepared a pitch deck to validate the direction before committing to the build.

The biggest surprise wasn't what I planned, it was what I deliberately left out.

Every feature I removed made the core idea stronger because customers don't need a tool that recommends everything, they need one they can trust to recommend the right thing.

The biggest lesson I took away wasn't about AI or software development, it was about product strategy.

Ambition isn't measured by how many features you build, it's measured by how well you solve one meaningful problem📌.

Sometimes saying "not yet" to a feature is the very decision that gives your product a chance to succeed.

Day 51/60 of my Claude AI Mastery Challenge.
Day 1/10 of building TeeTronics Gadgets Smart Match.

The journey from idea to a real product starts now.
