The toughest investor isn't the one sitting across the table, it's the one that refuses to let your assumptions slide.
That realization inspired this project.

For Day 50 of my ABTalks 60-Day Claude AI Mastery Challenge, I built Defend Your Experience, an AI-powered grant panel simulator designed to challenge founders the same way a real funding committee would.

Instead of creating another pitch deck, I built the panel that critiques it, and I fed the system the current reality of TeeTronics Gadgets:
SMEDAN registration.
Four completed business planning documents.
A business that's still pre-launch.
Zero sales.

Then I let the AI interview me as if I were defending my business before a grant committee.

The biggest surprise came from one simple statement I wrote:
"Students already trust us."
The AI immediately challenged it.
How do you know?
Where's the evidence?
Have customers actually bought from you?

That moment hit harder than I expected because the statement felt true but it wasn't yet provable.

So it reminded me that founders often speak from conviction, while investors listen for evidence.
That changed the way I think about pitching.

The strongest part of my business wasn't the vision I have written, it was the facts I could verify.
My SMEDAN registration.
My validation reports.
My planning documents.
Those were things no panel could argue with.
Everything else still has to be earned.

The biggest lesson I took away from this project wasn't about AI, it was about credibility.
Confidence can open the conversation✨.
Evidence is what keeps it going📌.

If you can't defend a claim with proof today, don't present it as a fact. Instead, present it as the milestone you're working toward.
That's not weakness, that's honesty.
And honesty builds far more trust than exaggeration ever will.

Day 50/60 of my Claude AI Mastery Challenge.

If someone challenged every claim you made about your project today, which ones could you confidently back up with evidence?
