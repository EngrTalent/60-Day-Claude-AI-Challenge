I used to rewrite the same AI prompts over and over again then I realized the problem wasn't the prompt, it was the process behind it.
That realization inspired this project

For Day 49 of my ABTalks 60-Day Claude AI Mastery Challenge, I built a Personal AI Playbook, an interactive system that helps you build prompts from reusable components instead of starting from a blank page every time.

Rather than saving hundreds of individual prompts, the playbook lets you assemble them using building blocks.
Define the role.
Set the objective.
Add constraints.
Choose the tone.
Include quality checks.

The prompt comes together naturally, and it can be adapted to almost any task without rewriting everything from scratch.
The biggest surprise wasn't the prompt builder itself, it was what happened before I even started building.

Instead of jumping straight into development, I began with a short interview to identify my biggest bottleneck.
The answer wasn't writing prompts, it was spending too much time cleaning up AI outputs afterward.
That single insight changed the direction of the entire project.
The goal stopped being "create better prompts," it became "design a system that consistently produces better results," and that shift made all the difference.

The biggest lesson I took away from this project wasn't about prompt engineering, it was about systems thinking.
Templates eventually become outdated.
Systems keep adapting.
A library of saved prompts grows larger over time.
A well-designed framework grows smarter.

And that's the approach I want to carry into every future AI workflow I build because the real productivity gain isn't saving one good prompt, it's eliminating the need to reinvent your thinking every single time.
