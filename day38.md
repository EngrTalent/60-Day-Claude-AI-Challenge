A lot would also think that better AI results came from writing longer prompts.
Guess what, today's project proved that wrong✨.

The biggest improvement came from changing the process, and not the prompt.

For Day 38 of my ABTalks 60-Day Claude AI Mastery Challenge, I built Typing Speed Studio, a full-featured typing platform with multiple typing modes, specialized content categories, live performance analytics, achievement tracking, and everything running offline in a single HTML file.

The most interesting part wasn't the finished product, it was what happened before Claude wrote a single line of code.
Instead of Claude jumping straight into development, I instructed Claude to slow down.
First, ask questions.
Understand the kind of experience I wanted.
Clarify what should be customizable.
Challenge generic design choices.
Only then should it start building.
That one decision changed everything.
The final result felt more intentional, more polished, and far closer to what I had imagined😉.

It reminded me that good prompting isn't just about describing the destination, it's about designing the journey.
When we give AI a better process to follow, we usually get better outcomes than simply asking for a bigger or more detailed result.
That's the biggest lesson I took away from today's project.

Great AI doesn't begin with better answers, it begins with better questions.
And sometimes, asking the model to pause and think before creating is the smartest prompt of all📌.
